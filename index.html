9-Algorithm Guide

1 Recency-weighted frequency

Counts historical appearances with exponential recency weighting. Recency curve controls how steeply recent draws outweigh older ones. Formula: w = (1+decay)^(i/N×3). Increase if the machine changed recently.

2 Gap Z-score (overdue)

Every number should appear every 9.833 draws in a fair 6/59 lottery. Formula: Z = (gap − 9.833) / √(9.833×(1−6/59)). Z > 1.5 = Overdue (purple ball). The most statistically grounded signal — the law of large numbers means absent numbers must converge toward expected frequency over time.

3 CDM Bayesian model — Academic

Compound-Dirichlet-Multinomial model from arXiv:2403.12836 (2024) — the only peer-reviewed academic paper published specifically for 6-number lottery prediction. Uses a Dirichlet prior over multinomial draw probabilities. Posterior expectation: E[p_n] = (α + count_n) / (Σα + N×6). α = prior strength. Higher α pulls toward uniform distribution. Increase α for older data where machine changes may have altered distributions.

4 Markov chain transitions

59×59 matrix: which numbers appeared in draw N+1 after each number in draw N. Scores candidates by transition probability from the last draw's numbers. Needs 200+ draws for a meaningful matrix.

5 Co-occurrence pairs

C(6,2)=15 pairs per draw. Tracks all 1,711 possible pairs. Numbers with higher total co-appearance activity score higher. Final set construction also maximises internal pair affinity — selecting numbers that historically appeared together.

6 4-partition combinatorial filter

Based on Lotterycodex research (2017–2026). Divides 59 numbers into LOW-ODD, LOW-EVEN, HIGH-ODD, HIGH-EVEN. Enforces: 3-odd/3-even (33% of draws), 3-high/3-low (34% of draws), sum within range, consecutive pair (~58% of draws), and all 4 partitions represented. These are mathematically proven structural properties of UK Lotto 6/59.

7 Day-of-week machine bias

Wednesday and Saturday use different machines and ball sets, producing subtle per-day frequency differences. Formula: bias = (target_rate − other_rate) / other_rate. Only active when Wednesday or Saturday is selected and CSV contains a Day column.

8 Positional order statistics

Based on Tse 2024 (Applied Mathematics). When draw numbers are sorted low-to-high, each position (1st–6th) follows a different hypergeometric distribution. Tracks per-position frequency per number and assigns positional bonuses. Needs 500+ draws.

9 Digital root + last-digit bias

Two sub-signals: (1) Digital root — groups 59 numbers into 9 families, underrepresented families get a Z-score bonus. (2) Last-digit bias — UK Lotto data shows non-uniform distribution of number endings 0–9; numbers with over-represented endings get a bonus. Needs 1,000+ draws.
